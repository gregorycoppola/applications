\section{Sparse Inference and Pruning}
\label{sec:efficiency}

The implicit factor graph appears to exhibit sparse effective dependency
structure: each node has only a few strong neighbors, and most conditional
relationships are weak or near-zero. If this structure can be identified
--- which SAE features matter for a given task, which AND connections are
active, which OR gates are relevant --- the rest can be skipped. This is
structured pruning with a theoretical justification, though the density
of internal superposition means the effective sparsity at the hidden-unit
level may be less than at the factor graph level.

\subsection*{Task-Specific Factor Graphs}

Different tasks activate different subgraphs of the implicit factor graph.
This is the theoretical foundation for mixture-of-experts and sparse
attention: they are approximations to task-specific factor graph routing,
and the BP framework gives a principled account of what they are
approximating.

\subsection*{Distillation as Factor Graph Compression}

Knowledge distillation can be understood as factor graph compression:
finding a smaller factor graph that approximates the posteriors of the
larger one. The BP framework suggests a principled objective for
distillation: minimize KL divergence between the posteriors of the teacher
and student factor graphs, not just between their output distributions.
